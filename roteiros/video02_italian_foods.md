> Psst. These three Italian foods Americans love? They're not what you think. And number one isn't even from Italy. Number three: pepperoni. Order a pepperoni pizza in Italy, and you might get bell peppers. Because in Italian, peperoni means peppers. The spicy salami Americans love was born in Italian-American butcher shops in New York, around nineteen nineteen. Number two: Fettuccine Alfredo. This one really is from Rome. A chef named Alfredo made it for his pregnant wife, with just pasta, butter and cheese. No cream. Then two Hollywood stars ate it on their honeymoon in nineteen twenty, and brought it home to America. Where cooks added heavy cream to thicken the sauce. In Italy, the simple version is just called pasta al burro: buttered pasta. And number one: Caesar salad. Not Julius. Not Rome. Not even Italy. It was invented in Tijuana, Mexico, in the nineteen twenties, by an Italian immigrant named Caesar Cardini. Legend says on a busy Fourth of July, the kitchen ran low, so he tossed in what he had. ## Fatos e fontes
- Pepperoni: criação ítalo-americana; primeira menção registrada em 1919, em Nova York; em italiano "peperoni" = pimentões.
  [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/1478605/american-origins-pepperoni/), [PMQ](https://www.pmq.com/who-invented-pepperoni-pizza-the-answer-is-complicated/)
- Fettuccine Alfredo: Alfredo di Lelio, Roma, para a esposa grávida; base é a "fettuccine al burro" (massa, manteiga, parmesão);
  Mary Pickford e Douglas Fairbanks na lua de mel em 1920; nos EUA, cozinheiros adicionaram creme de leite.
  [Wikipedia – Alfredo alla Scrofa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_alla_Scrofa), [Chowhound](https://www.chowhound.com/1696284/hollywood-fettucine-alfredo-history)
